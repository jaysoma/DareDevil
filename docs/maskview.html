What to look for

  • lsnr against the onsets. The amber line is DFN's own guess at speech-to-noise. Watch where it crosses the dashed 0 dB line relative to the white onset marks from the app's own onset detector; the grey ticks are when its gate decided.
  • Where the gain map lights up. Bright bands are what DFN kept. Across this walk the most-kept bands sit around 1–2.5 kHz, where speech carries its formants.
  • The phone talking. At 6.08 s and 14.36 s the phone itself says "Scanning heartbeat signature" and "Begin test recording". It's speech to DFN, from a speaker between the capsules.
  • A quiet tape. Across the walk, lsnr averages −4.4 dB and sits above 0 dB in 32% of frames. Jay's raw tapes sit around −48 to −51 dBFS, quiet enough that DFN partly treats them as noise.

Jay's bench notes

  • lsnr as a speech track worked on his house tapes: it crossed 0 dB at every speech start, within 16 ms of the MF onset, and never in gaps.
  • The mask as a speech track missed a soft first word and opened on footsteps.
  • Weighting the 5 ms angle read by the mask strengthened the ghost direction instead of the voice.
  • Nothing normalises the input level. On LOCATA, normalising raised lsnr from −5.4 to +3.3 dB.

Jay's caution: anything upstream in his pipeline could explain these. They're being re-tested on public datasets with independent truth.
