Two capsules · one 2022 phone · public benchmarks

DareDevil

What can two microphones on an old phone tell you about where people are speaking from, using only signal processing?

DareDevil is a live app that draws what a two-microphone phone hears: each sound where the pair heard it come from, speech as a bubble and everything else as rings. It's built to show the limits of the math honestly, in a room it has never heard, with nothing beyond the phone's own two capsules. These are its first results on public data with independent ground truth: 16 real rooms in STARSS23, tracked walking talkers in LOCATA, and labelled echoes in dEchorate.

  • Device Galaxy Z Flip4, unfolded
  • Pair 2 capsules, 157 mm
  • Capture raw, 48 kHz
  • Bearing GCC-PHAT and a precedence onset read
  • Author jaysoma

For researchers working on speaker orientation and localisation. In July, Turi, Politis, Sudarsanam and Virtanen published "Speaker head orientation estimation with a single microphone array using phase spectrogram features", motivated in part by a device telling which device a command is directed to. I'm working from the other end: a bearing from two capsules, in plain signal processing. One result below ties the two together. On LOCATA, at the phone's spacing, a talker alone and facing the array was never misread; alone and facing away, most reads were more than 10° off (Finding 3). Facing decides when a two-mic bearing can be trusted. Much of this was measured on STARSS23, from Tampere University and Sony. I'd value anyone's eyes on it, and on what survives when an array is cut down to a single pair.

FINDING 1 · STARSS23, MIC FORMAT, ALL 6 PAIRS

Pairwise GCC-PHAT on STARSS23 reads wide, and the rigid sphere accounts for all of it

On 1,422 single-talker speech onsets across 16 rooms, GCC-PHAT on each capsule pair of the MIC format leans away from 0°: about +5° at 30–45° and +19° at 45–60°. Pushing the same directions through the rigid-sphere response in the STARSS23 README, with no room at all, reproduces that lean band by band to within about 1°. Real rooms add essentially no lean to GCC-PHAT here. Diffraction around the baffle lengthens the delay between capsules, and the free-field formula reads that as a wider angle.

FINDING 2 · STARSS23, EVERY ONSET OF EVERY CLASS

When the bearing is wrong, it has usually locked onto another sound that was also there

Every labelled onset in 16 rooms was read through all six pairs: 44,988 reads. Three in four speech onsets had another labelled source active around them. Of GCC-PHAT's wrong speech reads (more than 10° off), 54% sit on the direction that filled the half-second before the onset, or on another labelled source, each at about 2.5 times its chance rate. That's two-thirds of its outliers. Most wrong reads are wrong from the start: 83% were already wrong at the first 43 ms draw.

FINDING 3 · LOCATA, TRACKED TALKERS, 160 MM PAIR

At the phone's spacing, a talker alone and facing the array was never misread

LOCATA's DICIT array has a pair 160 mm apart, against the phone's 157 mm, and OptiTrack gives each talker's head position and facing. On 341 onsets from walking talkers, GCC-PHAT missed by more than 10° on none of the 52 where the talker was alone and facing the array. The two causes of wrong reads were the other talker speaking during the read, and the talker facing away: with the direct sound turned away, the reflections take over.

FINDING 4 · PRECEDENCE READ, STARSS23 AND IMAGE-SOURCE ROOMS

A precedence read is pulled toward 0° when its gate opens late, and counting only clean, rising windows removes most of the pull

The onset read (3 ms windows, 1 ms apart; the first direction to recur wins) is within 1° in free field. In real rooms it read up to 19° nearer 0° than GCC-PHAT on the same cuts. Ablations in simulated rooms showed the cause: in noise, after a previous phrase's tail, or with the onset label off by up to 100 ms, the energy gate opens tens of ms into the word. By then the floor, ceiling, front-wall and back-wall images, all nearer 0° than the talker for a horizontal pair, are in every window. Counting only windows that are rising and dominated by one wave fixed most of it on the real cuts.

FINDING 5 · SCORED UNDER A STATED RULE

Accuracy of the working machinery, with every outlier set aside and catalogued, never hidden

Only true angles within ±75° are scored. A read off by more than 36° is set aside as an outlier, and a scenario (one room through one pair, one talker in one recording, or one dEchorate setup) whose remaining reads average more than 22.5° off is dropped whole. The share kept is always reported beside the score, and every outlier is catalogued by kind: mirror, end-fire pin, echo capture, another source, the pause's direction.

FINDING 6 · THE PHONE'S 0° IN SILENCE

The phone's pull toward 0° in pauses looks like a signal common to both channels, not the room

In the phone's own recordings, 44% of tracked directions during pauses sit within 6° of 0°, and the share rises with frame strength: 13% of the weakest quarter of pause frames, 30% of the strongest. In STARSS23's pauses, GCC-PHAT peaks land within 6° of 0° 10.5% of the time, against 9.5% for a null with no coherence between capsules, although low-frequency diffuse noise is present in every room (coherence 0.99 below 300 Hz). A controlled lab run with human speech then added one cause at a time. A signal identical in both channels produced the 0° pull (76–90% of GCC-PHAT's wrong reads under it), and diffuse noise almost never did (0–5%). That points at the phone's capture path; the test on its own audio is written and waiting.

Along the way: with two capsules, ODAS reports exactly 0.0° for any zero-delay win, because its scan sphere's first point is the pole and ties keep the first point.

Sources: results/zero_deg/report.md, results/batchA/lab/report.md
FINDING 7 · WHICH READS TO TRUST

The app needs to know which reads are right, and the clues are there, but none is enough alone

A live display doesn't need every read to be right. It needs to draw the right ones solid and dim the rest. On STARSS23 single-talker onsets, the two schemes agreeing within 5° with a clean onset picks out about a third of the reads, and those are much better than the rest.

The lab adds a caution. Under a steady directional source or a signal common to both channels, both schemes lock onto the same wrong direction, so agreement can't be the whole rule. In the lab, dimming any read within 10° of a strongly directional pause caught 83% of GCC-PHAT's wrong reads, and dimmed 10% of its right ones. On LOCATA, the best single clue, the margin between GCC-PHAT's first and second peaks, separates right from wrong at an AUC of 0.72. The rule will be chosen on one dataset, tested untouched on the other, and run once on the STARSS23 evaluation set.

FINDING 8 · WHAT DIDN'T WORK

Negative results, stated plainly

  • A scan's second and third peaks aren't a backup for the talker. When GCC-PHAT's first angle misses on STARSS23, its second or third peak lands on the talker 5.0% of the time, against 14.6% by chance.
  • The precedence read's "first reflection" isn't the reflection. On dEchorate's labelled echoes it hits the earliest distinct echo 16% of the time, against 12% by chance, and it arrives a median 3 ms after the onset in every wall setting, including all-absorbing. That's the window length, not the room.
  • Spatial aliasing caused no wrong locks in the lab, at 157 mm or 69 mm, and raising the analysis band's low edge only hurt, in the reflective room.
  • For relative range, only the direct-to-reverberant ratio is consistent. On LOCATA it orders 78% of pairs of reads twice as far apart, and its spread at one spot (1.3 dB) is about half the step. The first 5 ms of level is at chance (54%), and a cepstral echo lag finds the pitch period, not the floor.
Sources: starss23, dechorate, batchA/lab, batchA/locata
LOOK INSIDE

What DeepFilterNet3 keeps, frame by frame

DeepFilterNet3's enhance() returns only cleaned audio. Called directly, it also gives its 32-band gain mask and its own speech-to-noise estimate, lsnr. On the author's recordings, lsnr crossed 0 dB at each speech start, close to the app's onset detector, and DFN3's two-frame look-ahead makes it a speech gate about 30 ms behind live. On STARSS23 it's less tidy. As recorded, the files are too quiet for it and it calls nearly everything noise. With one fixed gain, in room 16 it crossed 0 dB within 0.6 s of 31 of 35 labelled speech onsets, but it also opened on a knock and on footsteps with nobody talking. NEXT

Planned, in order

  • Commit or dim: the simplest rule that predicts a correct read, chosen on STARSS23 and tested untouched on LOCATA, then the other way round, then run once on the STARSS23 evaluation set, which nothing has touched.
  • "That came off the wall": first-reflection angle and lag, from a direct-path-cancelling read, scored against dEchorate's labelled echoes.
  • Orientation from reflections: as a talker turns, the echoes don't move, but the faced wall's echo gets brighter. Planned on LOCATA's tracked head orientation.
  • Several talkers at once: streaming diarization to say who is speaking, with the bearing taken at each talker's own onsets.
