# Blur as the recogniser's struggle: first look, walk-215800 (2026-10-24)

Jay's idea: a decoder that only commits words it's sure of turns rotation's blur into something countable. The
measures are fewer words, stalls and giving up when the talker faces away. Data: Android SpeechRecognizer replaying
walk-215800 on the phone, with every partial timed on the tape clock (`recognizer.json`, `recognizer-partials.txt`,
Dictate `captures/geonar/walk-215800/`). Facing from the decode and `harness/rotation/facing_stops.json`; the CDR
contrast from `tapes/walk-215800/band_windows.json`.

## What the log shows
**The same callout at one spot (2 m), four facings:** "Rotated clockwise 90 degrees" each time.

| facing | Android's final | words right | longest wait for a new word |
|---|---|---|---|
| 90 | "Pathways, 90 degrees" | 2 of 4 | 0.47 s |
| 180 | "Ro." then no match | 0 | 1.43 s, then gave up |
| 270 | "Rotate the clockwise and I need grease" | 2 of 4, plus invented words | 0.51 s |
| 360 (facing) | "Go to the clockwise 90 degrees" | 3 of 4 | 0.51 s |

- **Facing the phone, the opening line** came out "Two meters right in front of the front facing the phone": nearly
  right, with a longest wait of 0.42 s.
- **The order is facing, then side, then away:** Jay's prediction. It is one take per facing.
- **The start doesn't move; the decoder stalls.** The first partial comes 0.42-0.52 s after speech begins at every
  facing. Facing away, it stalled for 1.43 s after one syllable and gave up.

**Against the CDR contrast, on the same utterances** (CDR 4-8 kHz minus 0.1-1.1 kHz, voiced windows only):

| outcome | utterances | contrast |
|---|---|---|
| decoded (a final with words) | 12 | +0.8 dB |
| gave up after partials | 7 | −0.5 dB (6 with voiced windows) |
| no partial at all | 23 | −1.7 dB (11 with voiced windows) |

The two instruments lean the same way, but they overlap widely, and the give-ups are few.

## Cautions (from the desktop agent's notes, kept)
- **No match alone isn't giving up.** Error 7 also fires every ~3 s while the recogniser's sessions cycle on room
  noise (31 times here, e.g. 82.2, 85.2, 88.2, 91.2 s). Only "partials, then no match" is giving up.
- **The noise floor isn't known yet.** Replay the same tape twice: whatever the recogniser does differently on an
  identical replay is the floor any rotation effect has to beat.
- **"Muddy" wasn't observed.** The failures at 44-124 s coincide with Whisper producing junk too ("Ro-2-993", "Road
  8993", "Yeah, yeah"). Turning can't be told apart there from odd speech, quiet speech or kitchen noise. The CDR
  column above is the independent check, and it's only a lean.

## Update: the same-phrase walks (`same_phrase.txt`, `harness/rotation/decode_blur/score_phrases.py`)
"There are six fresh beers in the fridge", 3-9 phrases per facing. Content words right (six, fresh, beers, fridge):

| facing | 2 m (231705) | 5 m (231921) | CDR contrast, 2 m / 5 m |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 81% (3) | 88% (6) | +1.6 / −2.5 dB |
| 90 | 92% (9) | 79% (6) | −1.7 / −3.3 |
| 135 | 69% (3) | 58% (3) | −2.2 / −4.3 |
| 180 | 100% (4) | 75% (5) | −3.1 / −4.0 |

- **At 2 m the recogniser doesn't track facing.** Facing away it decoded the phrase perfectly 4 times out of 4.
  walk-215800's one-take "180: gave up" doesn't replicate here.
- **At 5 m there's a lean:** facing 88%, turned 58-79%. But 90 and 180 are out of order and the takes are few.
- **Drops, corrections and stalls don't track facing** at either distance (longest wait 0.75-1.16 s throughout).
- **The CDR contrast tracks facing at both distances,** on the same utterances. The sound changed; the recogniser's
  output mostly didn't.
- **The errors are consonant swaps** (beers → piers, pears, gears, years, fears; fridge → bridge; six → sixth) at every
  facing, facing the phone included.
- **Untested:** that the language model fills a repeated, predictable phrase back in. A phrase it can't predict
  (unrelated words) would test that.
